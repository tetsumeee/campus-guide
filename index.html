01 — Concept

A tour guide that drives instead of pointing.

We built a scaled-down cardboard and foam model of our university campus and gave it a resident guide. A visitor walks up, asks where a building is, and the robot doesn't hand over a map. It leads the way.

Everything runs on the robot: ROS 2 on a Raspberry Pi 4 handles mapping and driving, a YOLOv8 model keeps it on course where the LiDAR can't see walls, and a language model turns a spoken question into a navigation goal.

02 — How it works

From a question to an arrival in four stages.

Each stage is a separate piece of the system, so you can watch every one of them on the dashboard while the robot moves.

01ASK

Ask

Talk to the robot's USB microphone, or scan the QR code on the chassis and speak or type on your phone.

in  voice · text
out utf-8 string, ko-KR / en-US
02PARSE

Understand

The Python server sends the text to an LLM, which pulls out the destination and writes a short spoken reply.

in  free-form question
out {target, response}
03DRIVE

Drive

The target maps to (X, Y) on the saved SLAM map. Nav2 plans a route and drives it, steering around feet and dropped pens.

in  NavigateToPose(x, y, θ)
out /cmd_vel
04ARRIVE

Explain

Once the camera confirms arrival, gTTS reads out a description of the building and the TFT screen updates.

in  goal reached
out USB speaker · 2.0" TFT
# one request, end to end
user  ▸ "경영대학 어디에 있나요?"                      // web speech, ko-KR
llm   ▸ {"target": "business_dept", "response": "Follow me to the Business building!"}
nav2  ▸ NavigateToPose  x=2.10  y=0.85  θ=1.57   // map: campus.pgm
yolo  ▸ business_dept  conf=0.91  ex=+12px  h=0.64H  → ARRIVED
tts   ▸ gTTS(lang="ko") → usb-speaker  "경영대학에 도착했습니다…"
03 — Navigation

Mapped once by hand. Driven by itself after that.

3.1SLAM + Nav2

A 2D map of the campus, drawn by the LiDAR.

We drive the robot around the cardboard campus once from a laptop keyboard. slam_toolbox turns the 360° scans into an occupancy grid, which we save as a .pgm map.

After that, every building is just a coordinate. Nav2 plans a path on the map, follows it, and replans when something steps in the way.

  • mappingslam_toolbox, teleop over keyboard
  • map formatcampus.pgm + campus.yaml
  • plannerNav2 global + local costmaps
  • goalsone (x, y, θ) per building
3.2Open-space fix

When the LiDAR goes blind, the camera steers.

In the middle of an open square the LiDAR's beams don't reach any wall, so the robot loses its position and drifts. We fixed this with natural landmark tracking.

A lightweight YOLOv8 model, trained on our cardboard buildings, runs on the Pi Camera. It gives two signals that are simple enough to control on.

  • steeringtarget drifts left of centre → steer left until ex ≈ 0
  • distancebounding box grows as we approach; tall enough = arrived
  • modelYOLOv8n, custom classes per building
04 — Interface

Two ways to talk to it. One big red button.

Both modes run in parallel, so a visitor can speak to the robot directly while another person drives the demo from a phone.

Mode AHands-free

Onboard voice

Stand in front of the robot and ask out loud. No phone, no app, nothing to install.

  • Onboard USB microphone
  • Answers through the USB speaker
  • Status on the 2.0" TFT, e.g. "Driving to Cafe"
Mode BBYOD · QR

Phone web app

Scan the QR code on the chassis. A lightweight Flask/FastAPI + HTML5 page opens on your phone.

  • Web Speech API voice input, or type in noisy halls
  • Instant toggle between 한국어 and ENG
  • Virtual joystick for manual override during demos

E-Stop on every screen. One tap publishes /cmd_vel = 0 and locks autonomous driving until it's cleared.
